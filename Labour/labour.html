Research Question 1

Employment Status

What was the distribution of employment status among employed respondents between 2022 and 2024?

Key Finding

Self-employment remained the dominant form of employment throughout the survey period. Agricultural self-employment increased from 29.69% in 2022 to 34.16% in 2024, while non-agricultural self-employment also recorded modest growth.

Paid employment remained relatively stable at approximately 14–15%, while agricultural family work declined over the period.

Overall, employment growth occurred mainly through informal and self-employed activities rather than formal wage employment.

Research Question 2

Employment Sectors

What were the major employment sectors among employed respondents between 2022 and 2024?

Key Finding

The Private Informal sector consistently accounted for approximately 90% of employment across the survey period.

Public Government and Private Formal employment each represented approximately 5%, while NGOs, religious organisations, international organisations and other sectors collectively represented less than 1%.

The distribution indicates that Ghana's labour market remained overwhelmingly dominated by private informal employment, with limited expansion of formal employment.

Research Question 3

Major Occupations

What were the major occupations of employed respondents between 2022 and 2024?

Key Finding

Skilled agricultural, forestry and fishery workers consistently represented the largest occupational group, accounting for more than half of employed respondents.

Service and sales occupations increased gradually, while craft and related trades workers consistently represented approximately 11% of employment.

Professional occupations accounted for only about 4–5%, while managers, technicians and clerical workers represented relatively small shares of employment.

Research Question 4

Employment-Related Benefits

To what extent did employed respondents receive employment-related benefits between 2022 and 2024?

Key Finding

Employment contracts were the most widely available employment benefit among eligible respondents.

Social security, paid leave and paid sick leave were received by less than half of eligible workers, while employer-sponsored health insurance and medical benefits were the least accessible benefits.

The findings indicate that although some workers had formal employment arrangements, access to comprehensive employment protections remained limited.

Research Summary

Overall Labour Market Findings

Combined interpretation of the four labour-market research questions across the 2022–2024 AHIES survey period.

Overall Conclusion

The combined findings indicate that Ghana's labour market between 2022 and 2024 remained predominantly informal, self-employment driven and agriculture-based. While employment participation remained relatively stable, there was limited evidence of structural transformation toward formal employment or widespread improvements in employment quality.

Expanding formal job opportunities, strengthening worker protections and promoting economic diversification remain important priorities for sustainable labour market development.
